Mission 3 · 25 minutes

📚 Reading & Writing Studio: My World of Interests

Read a model, notice how the writer develops ideas, practise present-simple forms, and create a paragraph of your own.

1. Read the model

A person's world of interests

There are so many things I love to do, but I am sure I love singing. It is, without a doubt, my favourite activity. I sing horribly, but I still like to do it when I am driving or when I am in the shower. I also like doing exercise. I love waking up early and smelling the cold morning air. I love going out for a morning run and feeling the cold air on my face. I don’t like running when it is hot, for example, during summer, but I love feeling my legs exhausted after a few kilometres.

There are many other things I don’t like when I am running. I don't like watching for cars when I cross the streets because it makes me slower.

Notice: The text includes some more advanced forms. Today, focus on the present-simple verbs and the way the writer adds examples, reasons, and details. You do not need to master every structure in this text.

2. Reading detective 🔎

Answer the questions. Then compare with a partner.

1. What is the writer’s favourite activity?
2. Name one thing the writer likes doing.
3. When does the writer dislike running?
4. Why doesn't the writer like watching for cars?
Pair discussion: Which activities do you have in common with the writer? Which activities are different for you?

Remember: he/she/it takes -s/-es in affirmative sentences. For negatives, use don't/doesn't + base verb.

4. Create your own paragraph ✍️

Write 8–10 sentences about your interests and your family. Use the model to help you, but make the ideas your own.

❤️ My favourite activity

I really like… / My favourite activity is…

🎧 More interests

I also enjoy… / I love…

🚫 Something I dislike

I don't like… because…

👨‍👩‍👧 Family

My mother likes… / My brother doesn't like…

🕒 When / with whom

I do it when… / I do it with…

🔗 Connect ideas

and · but · because · for example

Sample answer: “There are many things I like to do, but my favourite activity is listening to music. I enjoy listening to music when I travel by bus. I also love watching movies with my family. I don't like playing tennis because it is difficult for me. My brother likes playing soccer, but I don't enjoy it very much. My mother loves cooking, and my father enjoys watching football. On weekends, we like spending time together.”
➡️ Transition: You now have ideas about your own interests and your family. Next, work with a partner to turn these ideas into a natural conversation.
Mission 4 · 30 minutes

🗣️ Conversation Studio: Let's Get to Know Each Other

Work in pairs. Create a conversation between two people meeting at a club, course, or social event. Use the language from today's lesson.

Conversation checklist

  • Start with a greeting and introduction.
  • Include at least four questions with do/does.
  • Include affirmative and negative answers.
  • Ask about one family member's interests.
  • React naturally: Really? That's interesting! Me too! Not me!
  • Finish the conversation naturally.

Build your dialogue

🌟 Sample conversation

Alex: Hi! I'm Alex. What do you like doing in your free time?
Sam: I enjoy playing basketball and listening to music. What about you?
Alex: I love listening to music, too, but I don't like basketball.
Sam: Really? Does your family enjoy sports?
Alex: Yes, they do. My dad loves football, but my sister doesn't like sports at all.
Sam: That's interesting! Do you enjoy watching movies?
Alex: Yes, I do. I love comedies. What kind of movies do you like?
Sam: I like action movies. Maybe we can watch one sometime!

🎭 Rehearse and perform

Practise twice. The first time, use your notes. The second time, look up more often and try to sound natural. Then present your conversation to the class.

Peer feedback: Did they ask clear questions? Did they use do/does correctly? Did they answer in complete sentences? Did they talk about a family member?
➡️ Transition: You’ve used the language to communicate. Let’s finish with a short challenge to show what you can do independently.
